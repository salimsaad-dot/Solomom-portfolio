About the School

Butumagyebu S.T.M.A Primary/JHS was built in 1969 in the Ketan-Sekondi District. In 1968 the land was a coconut farm. The elders of the community, Egya Baake and Egya Basintwe, together with the chief of the land, Nana Koie I, pleaded for a school to be built in the town. It began as a temporary wooden structure for nursery pupils only.

The school officially started in 1969 under its first headteacher, Mr F. T. Mensah, with the motto “Gye Nyame”. Over time it became known for strong results in academics and sports, which encouraged more parents to enrol their children. Today it has about 300 learners from Basic 1 to JHS 3.

My Responsibilities

  • Preparing lesson plans and teaching assigned Science topics.
  • Selecting and making teaching and learning materials.
  • Asking questions to check understanding and giving class exercises.
  • Marking learners' work and giving feedback.
  • Managing classroom time and keeping professional conduct.
  • Working with my mentor and colleagues, and taking part in school activities.

Goals of My Internship

  • Develop confidence and competence in planning and delivering lessons.
  • Use learner-centred methods and suitable teaching and learning materials.
  • Improve classroom management, communication and time management.
  • Assess learners' understanding and use feedback to guide further teaching.
  • Reflect on my teaching and respond constructively to advice from my mentor and supervisor.

Classroom Rules

The rules in my Basic 7 classroom, and why they matter.

Come to Class on Time

Helps lessons begin promptly. Lateness is recorded, and repeat lateness is discussed with the learner and, if needed, a parent.

Raise Your Hand to Speak

Lets everyone contribute without speaking over one another. Turns are given fairly.

Respect Everyone

Creates a safe classroom where learners feel comfortable asking questions and making mistakes.

Care for the Classroom

A clean classroom supports health and helps learning materials last longer.

Challenges & Solutions

Challenges I met while teaching, and how I handled them.

Time Management

Lessons sometimes ran longer when learners needed extra explanation. I prioritised the main objectives, estimated time for each stage and kept a short closing activity ready.

Different Learning Needs

Learners differed in prior knowledge, confidence and pace. I used clear explanations, varied examples, guided practice and extra questions, and asked my mentor for advice when needed.

Limited Teaching Resources

Where materials were not available, I planned ahead and made safe, low-cost alternatives such as cardboard models, simple drawings and demonstrations.

Participation & Management

I gave concise instructions, organised transitions carefully and consistently reinforced positive participation.

Working with Others

I communicated respectfully with my mentor and colleagues, asked for clarification when necessary and used their advice to improve my practice.

We discussed lesson objectives, reviewed lesson plans, shared teaching resources and talked through classroom challenges.
